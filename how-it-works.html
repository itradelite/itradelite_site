How it works

Everything the system does, in plain words. The browser only displays published data and does the position-size arithmetic; all trading logic runs in the nightly pipeline.

1. Schedule (Sydney time, ASX trading days only)

  • ~19:00 — night scan. After the close, daily bars for the universe are downloaded and checked for freshness and coverage. If coverage is below 90% or the index bar is stale, recommendations are withheld (the Today page says so). Otherwise it publishes up to two recommended orders valid for the next trading day, plus setups and the full watchlist.
  • ~10:20, ~12:30, ~15:00 — live checks. The picks valid for today are re-checked against 15-minute bars: fill status, trailing level and a verdict. You can also trigger a check from the Live check page.
  • During the session — intraday scanner. Separate, shorter-horizon signals on 15m / 30m / 1h bars (Intraday), runnable on demand.
  • After the night scan — metrics. The track record replays every recommended order ever published.

Each data file carries a fresh until time. When it passes without a new run, every page shows a STALE banner; a health banner appears when any job is late or failing. Times are displayed in Brisbane time (AEST, no daylight saving); during daylight saving Sydney is one hour ahead.

2. Strategies

Trend pullback

Buys a dip toward short-term moving averages inside an established uptrend.

Momentum breakout

Buys strength through a recent high on volume, using STOP or STOP-LIMIT orders so it only enters if the breakout happens.

Mean reversion

Buys short-term oversold conditions (RSI(2), Bollinger band) expecting a snap-back, not a new trend.

Post-earnings drift

Follows the direction of the last earnings surprise for a short window.

52-week-high momentum

Buys stocks pushing to new 52-week highs.

Gap fade / continuation

Fades an overnight gap down or rides a gap up, depending on context.

Baseline (random entry)

Not a live strategy: a random-entry control used only in the Strategy decay audit to show what luck alone looks like under the same rules.

Whether any of these has real edge is tested separately on Strategy decay. At the last run, trend pullback and mean reversion showed no edge distinguishable from noise, and the newer strategies had too few trades to judge.

3. Market regime gate

The regime compares the ASX200 index with its 50- and 200-day moving averages.

  • Risk-on: all strategies may produce recommendations.
  • Risk-off: trend, momentum and breakout style strategies are gated off. Mean reversion is still allowed, so risk-off nights can still have picks (often all mean reversion).

4. Orders

  • LIMIT: buy only at or below the limit price.
  • STOP: buy once price trades up through the trigger.
  • STOP-LIMIT: like STOP, but never pay more than the limit; if the stock gaps above the limit the order is not filled.
  • Market on open: buy at the opening price.

Entry ref is the worst-case fill price (the limit for STOP-LIMIT) and is what sizing uses. Prices are shown to the ASX tick: 3 decimals under A$2.00, 2 decimals otherwise. At most two orders are recommended per night and never two for the same stock.

5. Position sizing

risk budget = portfolio × risk % risk qty = floor( risk budget / |entry ref − stop| ) position cap= floor( portfolio × max position % / entry ref ) cash cap = floor( portfolio / entry ref ) qty = min( risk qty, position cap, cash cap ) actual risk = qty × |entry ref − stop|

Example: A$100,000 portfolio, 1% risk (A$1,000), entry 10.00, stop 9.80. Risk qty = 1,000 / 0.20 = 5,000 shares (A$50,000). With max position 25% the cap is 2,500 shares (A$25,000), so the order is 2,500 (cap) and the actual risk is A$500, half the budget. The Today page shows (cap) and the actual A$ risk whenever a cap binds. Rows without an entry price are not sized.

6. Exits

Every trade starts with the strategy’s initial stop. If the stock opens below the stop, the exit is at the open (gap-through), not at the stop price.

After entry, the position is managed by an exit policy, shown on each pick in words, for example “Trail 2% below highest price since entry, max 20 days”. Policies compared in the track record:

  • Fixed 5 days (the old rule): exit at the close of day 5.
  • Percentage trail (1%, 2%, 3%): exit when price falls that far below the highest price since entry.
  • ATR trail (1× or 2× ATR): the same, with the distance set by recent volatility.
  • Trail after +1R: keep the initial stop until the trade is up one risk unit, then trail.
  • All trailing policies also have a time stop (20 trading days).

The default policy is chosen from the simulation evidence, which uses hourly bars where available, and is marked on the track record. Targets (TP1 / TP2) are reference levels from ATR, not automatic exits.

7. Signals and context

Each row shows a TA score, a composite score, sentiment (news, insider trades, search trends) and the next earnings date. A sentiment component that failed or had no data is shown as such and is left out of the score; it is never treated as neutral. Stocks too close to earnings are suppressed from recommendations; an unknown earnings date is flagged.

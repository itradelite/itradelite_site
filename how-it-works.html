How it works

Everything the system does, in plain words. The browser only displays published data and does the position-size arithmetic; all trading logic runs in the nightly pipeline.

1. Schedule (Sydney time, ASX trading days only)

  • ~19:00 — night scan. After the close, daily bars for the universe are downloaded and checked for freshness and coverage. If coverage is below 90% or the index bar is stale, recommendations are withheld (the Today page says so). Otherwise it publishes up to two recommended orders valid for the next trading day, plus setups and the full watchlist.
  • ~15:15 — pre-close scan. The same strategies run on today's bar with the latest live price spliced in, about 45 minutes before the close. Up to two orders to buy in the closing auction or at market before 15:59 Sydney (14:59 Brisbane during daylight saving; one minute before an early close). See Pre-close picks and section 8.
  • ~10:20, ~12:30, ~15:00 — live checks. The picks valid for today are re-checked against 15-minute bars: fill status, trailing level and a verdict. You can also trigger a check from the Live check page.
  • During the session — intraday scanner. Separate, shorter-horizon signals on 15m / 30m / 1h bars (Intraday), runnable on demand.
  • After the night scan — metrics. The track record replays every recommended order ever published.

Each data file carries a fresh until time. When it passes without a new run, every page shows a STALE banner; a health banner appears when any job is late or failing. Times are displayed in Brisbane time (AEST, no daylight saving); during daylight saving Sydney is one hour ahead.

2. Strategies

Trend pullback

Buys a dip toward short-term moving averages inside an established uptrend.

Momentum breakout

Buys strength through a recent high on volume, using STOP or STOP-LIMIT orders so it only enters if the breakout happens.

Mean reversion

Buys short-term oversold conditions (RSI(2), Bollinger band) expecting a snap-back, not a new trend.

Post-earnings drift

Follows the direction of the last earnings surprise for a short window.

52-week-high momentum

Buys stocks pushing to new 52-week highs.

Gap fade / continuation

Fades an overnight gap down or rides a gap up, depending on context.

Baseline (random entry)

Not a live strategy: a random-entry control used only in the Strategy decay audit to show what luck alone looks like under the same rules.

Whether any of these has real edge is tested separately on Strategy decay. At the last run, trend pullback and mean reversion showed no edge distinguishable from noise, and the newer strategies had too few trades to judge.

3. Market regime gate

The regime compares the ASX200 index with its 50- and 200-day moving averages.

  • Risk-on: all strategies may produce recommendations.
  • Risk-off: trend, momentum and breakout style strategies are gated off. Mean reversion is still allowed, so risk-off nights can still have picks (often all mean reversion).

4. Choosing the two orders (ranking)

Every setup that passes all its gates gets a composite score: 55% technical score + 25% fundamentals (v2) + 20% sentiment. A missing fundamentals or sentiment score is left out and the other weights are scaled up; it is never counted as neutral. This is ranking option1_v1 (since 25 Nov 2026), used by both the night and the pre-close scan.

  • Fundamentals v2 compares each company with its sector peers across the ASX universe on five pillars: valuation, cash generation, quality, balance sheet and growth (0–100, higher is better; ETFs are not scored).
  • Fundamentals filter: a stock whose v2 score is in the bottom 20% of that night’s scanned universe cannot be a recommended order. It still appears as a setup, marked excluded: fundamentals bottom 20% with its score and the cutoff. A stock with no fundamentals data is not penalised. The pre-close scan uses the cutoff from the previous night’s universe.
  • Trend / momentum tiebreak: when two trend, momentum, breakout, gap or earnings-drift setups are within 3 points of each other, the one with the stronger growth pillar ranks first. Mean-reversion setups are not affected. When this decides an order, the order card says so.
  • Then the top two are taken: never two orders for the same stock and at most one per sector class.
  • If fundamentals cannot be computed for most of the universe on a run, the ranking falls back to technical + sentiment only, with no filter or tiebreak, and the page shows Ranking DEGRADED.

The previous fundamentals score (v1) is still shown for information but no longer affects the ranking. Because the backtest could not separate the options statistically, the alternatives — the old ranking, v2 at 25% without the filter, technical only, and technical + sentiment — are tracked side by side on the track record (“Ranking variants”), re-picked every session from the same candidates, so the choice can be revisited on live results.

5. Orders

  • LIMIT: buy only at or below the limit price.
  • STOP: buy once price trades up through the trigger.
  • STOP-LIMIT: like STOP, but never pay more than the limit; if the stock gaps above the limit the order is not filled.
  • Market on open: buy at the opening price.
  • Market on close (pre-close picks): buy in the closing auction, or at market before the close. Entry ref is the live price (about 20 min delayed) rounded up to a valid ASX tick; the real fill is the closing price.

Entry ref is the worst-case fill price (the limit for STOP-LIMIT) and is what sizing uses. Prices are shown to the ASX tick: 3 decimals under A$2.00, 2 decimals otherwise. At most two orders are recommended per night and never two for the same stock.

6. Position sizing

risk budget = portfolio × risk % risk qty = floor( risk budget / |entry ref − stop| ) position cap= floor( portfolio × max position % / entry ref ) cash cap = floor( portfolio / entry ref ) qty = min( risk qty, position cap, cash cap ) actual risk = qty × |entry ref − stop|

Example: A$100,000 portfolio, 1% risk (A$1,000), entry 10.00, stop 9.80. Risk qty = 1,000 / 0.20 = 5,000 shares (A$50,000). With max position 25% the cap is 2,500 shares (A$25,000), so the order is 2,500 (cap) and the actual risk is A$500, half the budget. The Today page shows (cap) and the actual A$ risk whenever a cap binds. Rows without an entry price are not sized.

7. Exits

Every trade starts with the strategy’s initial stop. If the stock opens below the stop, the exit is at the open (gap-through), not at the stop price.

After entry, the position is managed by an exit policy. Three policies are tracked side by side on every pick, and each pick lists all three in plain words:

  • Fixed 5 days — the official default: “Sell at close of day 5 (by Mon 30 Nov)”.
  • Trail 2×ATR: “Trail 2×ATR = A$X below the highest price since entry” (A$X is twice the 14-day average true range at the signal).
  • Trail 1%: “Trail 1% below the highest price since entry”.

The Live check shows, for each filled pick, every policy's trail level or exit-by date and whether it says HOLD or EXIT; the main verdict always follows the default. The track record reports all three so you can see which would have done best. The wider set of policies also compared in the simulation:

  • Fixed 5 days (the old rule): exit at the close of day 5.
  • Percentage trail (1%, 2%, 3%): exit when price falls that far below the highest price since entry.
  • ATR trail (1× or 2× ATR): the same, with the distance set by recent volatility.
  • Trail after +1R: keep the initial stop until the trade is up one risk unit, then trail.
  • All trailing policies also have a time stop (20 trading days).

The default stays fixed 5 days unless the simulation (hourly bars where available) shows another policy is better by a margin that is unlikely to be luck; it is marked on the track record. Targets (TP1 / TP2) are reference levels from ATR, not automatic exits.

8. How the track record is reported

The headline is the model you trade: a fixed A$25,000 on every signal. Every recommended order is taken, capital is assumed to be sufficient (no “max two positions” or position-size caps), and the A$ results are simply added up — no compounding. The headline number is the additive return: the sum of every trade's net % on its stake, after margin interest under your funding model — your own capital (one A$25,000 stake by default) pays for the first open trade and every further trade open at the same time is bought on margin at 9% p.a., charged per day held. The A$ P&L after interest sits beside it, and a risk note says plainly when peak borrowing would exceed the ~70% loan-to-value most ASX margin lenders allow, or when the worst drawdown would be over half of your own capital (margin-call territory). Alongside the total P&L it shows the peak capital needed (the most positions open at once × A$25,000) and the return on that peak capital. A toggle shows the same for all setups, not only the recommended orders.

A secondary section keeps the older view: one A$100,000 account with risk-% sizing and caps, compared with buying an ASX200 ETF.

9. Night picks vs pre-close picks

Night signals use the completed daily bar, so they can only be traded at the next open — by then everyone running similar scans has the same signals. The pre-close scan exists to test whether buying before today's close is better. It is tracked as two streams next to the night picks:

  • Pre-close · historical estimate: the past night signals replayed as if bought at the signal day's close (closing price plus slippage). An honest proxy, because the pre-close scan itself only exists from now on.
  • Pre-close · forward tracking: the real pre-close picks, filled at that session's close, collected from 25 Nov 2026. It will show “n = 0” for a while.

A paired comparison (each signal compared with itself under both entry methods) says whether the difference is consistent or could still be noise. The pre-close scan withholds its picks when the market is not open, there are under 15 minutes to the close, data coverage is below 90% or live prices are more than 30 minutes old (Yahoo ASX prices are about 20 minutes delayed).

10. Signals and context

Each row shows a TA score, a composite score (see section 4), fundamentals (v2 with its percentile, v1 for information), sentiment (news, insider trades, search trends) and the next earnings date. A sentiment component that failed or had no data is shown as such and is left out of the score; it is never treated as neutral. Stocks too close to earnings are suppressed from recommendations; an unknown earnings date is flagged.
